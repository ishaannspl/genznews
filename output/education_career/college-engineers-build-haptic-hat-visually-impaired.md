## The Hook

Forget bloated gadgets that demand subscriptions and constant cloud access. Five college seniors just proved you can build life-changing assistive hardware using paper towel sensor tech and a baseball cap.

## TL;DR (Quick Hits)

- Five Rose-Hulman engineering undergrads built an open-source smart cap that detects obstacles for visually impaired users.
- The device runs for 12 hours on a single charge and operates completely offline without phones or Wi-Fi.
- The project adapts airport paper towel dispenser LiDAR sensors into a forehead haptic feedback system.

## The Breakdown

Senior design projects usually end up gathering dust on a hard drive, but five Rose-Hulman Institute of Technology students built something with immediate utility. Partnering with alum John Marum, founder of the non-profit AuralVision, the team tackled the blind spots that white canes and guide dogs miss, specifically head-height and hanging obstacles.

The setup mounts time-of-flight LiDAR sensors directly onto a baseball cap brim. When an object enters the user's path, internal haptic motors vibrate against the forehead to signal distance and direction. The entire system is self-contained on an integrated chip with a 12-hour battery life, cutting out smartphones, apps, and data plans entirely.

After testing prototypes with assisted living residents aged 70 to 99, the team learned that sensory inputs must adapt across age groups. Constant buzzes that feel normal to screen-native students caused headaches for older testers, prompting recommendations to make future vibration patterns customizable.

## Why It Matters

Big Tech routinely locks basic accessibility features behind four-figure hardware and closed software ecosystems. Keeping this design open-source and offline proves that early-career engineers can build low-cost, repairable tools that solve real-world physical barriers without extracting user data.
