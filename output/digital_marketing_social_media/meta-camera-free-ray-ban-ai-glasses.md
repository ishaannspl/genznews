## The Hook

Meta is finally ditching the cameras on its newest Ray-Ban smart glasses to escape the well-earned 'creeper' reputation of its previous hardware. If you have been waiting for smart eyewear that will not freak out everyone in the room, this $349 audio-only drop is aiming right for your vibe.

## TL;DR (Quick Hits)

- Meta announced camera-free Ray-Ban Audio glasses starting at $349, designed to dodge privacy backlash.
- The audio-only specs weigh just 43 grams, last up to 12 hours on a charge, and connect to Meta's new Muse AI assistant.
- Meta also dropped Gen 3 camera glasses with upgraded 3K video, plus new styles featuring pop star Lisa.

## The Breakdown

At its Connect 2026 conference, Meta unveiled the Ray-Ban Meta Audio glasses in response to harsh criticism over people using camera-equipped specs to covertly record strangers. By stripping out the camera entirely, the hardware gets a slimmer profile, weighing only 43 grams with a battery life stretching up to 12 hours.

These audio-first frames let you listen to music, take calls, and talk to Meta's Muse AI assistant without making anyone nearby suspicious. Alongside the audio version, Meta also refreshed its camera-enabled lineup with new Aviator and Zena styles, a 12 MP camera supporting 3K video, and a high-profile brand partnership with singer Lisa.

## Why It Matters

Wearable tech lives or dies by social acceptance, and nobody wants to hang out with someone wearing walking surveillance gear. Moving toward a lightweight, privacy-friendly audio format makes smart glasses feel less like a dystopian gadget and more like daily streetwear.
